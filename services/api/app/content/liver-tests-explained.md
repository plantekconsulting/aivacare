## ALT and AST
ALT (alanine aminotransferase) and AST (aspartate aminotransferase) are enzymes released when liver cells are irritated or injured. In fatty liver disease, a rising ALT often reflects more liver fat and inflammation. Common reasons ALT goes up include weight gain, less physical activity, more sugar or refined carbohydrates, alcohol, some medicines and supplements, and poorly controlled blood sugar. A typical upper limit of normal for ALT is about 40 U/L, though labs vary.

## Why ALT can rise even when you feel fine
Liver enzymes can change before you notice any symptoms. A rise is a signal to look at recent changes in diet, activity, sleep and alcohol, not a reason to panic. Your care team reviews trends and will contact you if action is needed.

## Platelets
Platelets are blood cells that help clotting. When the liver has significant scarring, platelet counts can slowly fall. Platelets are part of the FIB-4 score.

## FIB-4 score
FIB-4 is calculated from your age, AST, ALT and platelet count. It estimates the chance of significant liver scarring. Below 1.3 means low risk. Between 1.3 and 2.67 is "indeterminate" and may need another test. Above 2.67 means higher risk, and your hepatologist will usually recommend a scan such as elastography (FibroScan). FIB-4 is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

## HbA1c
HbA1c reflects your average blood sugar over about three months. Better blood sugar control helps reduce liver fat. Many people with MASLD aim for an HbA1c below 7%, but your target is set by your care team.
